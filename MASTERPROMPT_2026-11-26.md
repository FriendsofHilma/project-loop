## STEP 7 — INTERPERSONAL INTEGRATION

**Purpose: Understand the relationships between stakeholders and discover opportunities for cooperation.**

At this stage, we move beyond looking at the individual components of a project and start understanding the relationships between the people and organisations involved.

Identify and analyse:

1. Important individuals participating in or influencing the project.
2. Organisations, institutions, foundations, associations and research networks.
3. Relationships between these individuals and organisations.
4. Existing collaborations and historical relationships.
5. How different stakeholders conceptualise their relationships with one another.
6. Their respective interests, objectives, capabilities and contributions.
7. How they communicate, share knowledge and coordinate activities.
8. Potential differences in terminology, priorities, expectations and institutional culture.
9. Opportunities for collaboration, mutual support and shared initiatives.

An important distinction:

Do not merely describe who the stakeholders are. Try to understand how they understand one another.

For example, two organisations might be pursuing very similar objectives but describe their work using entirely different conceptual frameworks.

Our task is to identify these points of convergence and discover how communication can bridge them.

### Expected outputs

- Stakeholder relationship map.
- Individual and organisational profiles.
- Collaboration and dependency map.
- Communication opportunities.
- Potential synergies and conflicts.
- Opportunities for *samverkan*.
- Suggested introductions, conversations and joint activities.

The result should be a relational understanding of the project ecosystem.

This step should also maintain a distinction between verified relationships, reported relationships and hypotheses about potential relationships.

## STEP 8 — STRATEGIC COORDINATION

**Purpose: Translate our understanding into a time-bound strategy for action.**

Step 8 builds upon the interpersonal integration developed in step 7.

Having understood the stakeholders and their relationships, we now consider what should happen, when it should happen, and who should be involved.

This is where we formulate concrete strategies.

For each relevant stakeholder or collaboration opportunity, consider:

- What do we want to accomplish?
- Why is this collaboration valuable?
- What should happen next?
- Who should initiate the communication?
- Which other stakeholders should be involved?
- What resources are needed?
- What deadlines or important dates apply?
- What should be prepared beforehand?
- How will we know whether progress has been made?

### Time-bound strategic planning

Develop actionable plans with:

- Immediate actions.
- Short-term objectives.
- Medium-term objectives.
- Longer-term opportunities.
- Dependencies and milestones.
- Responsible individuals or organisations.
- Follow-up dates.

Distinguish between confirmed commitments, proposed activities and speculative opportunities.

### Example: Klirrhack 2027 and the Hilma Research Network

We are preparing for Klirrhack 2027 and considering a conference in the spring involving the Hilma Research Network.

Investigate whether these initiatives could complement one another.

Questions include:

- What is DFRI planning for its spring activities?
- What is the purpose and expected audience of Klirrhack?
- What is the Hilma Research Network hoping to accomplish?
- Could the conferences share participants, programme elements, speakers or infrastructure?
- Could a joint session or collaborative research workshop be organised?
- What needs to happen now to make such cooperation possible?

Do not assume that cooperation has already been agreed upon.

Instead, develop a realistic strategy for exploring and establishing it.

### Expected outputs

- Time-bound stakeholder action plan.
- Collaboration proposals.
- Communication drafts where appropriate.
- Calendar of relevant activities.
- Prioritised next actions.
- Milestones and follow-up mechanisms.

Step 8 should turn relational understanding into practical coordination.

# 5. New research objective: The Riemann hypothesis and the Cosmic Block

A particularly important new research objective is to investigate whether the mathematical structures of the Cosmic Block can help us explain and visualise the Riemann hypothesis.

This should become a dedicated research workstream.

### A. Establish the mathematical foundations

Begin by researching:

- The distribution of prime numbers.
- The Riemann zeta function.
- The Euler product.
- The analytic continuation of the zeta function.
- The functional equation.
- The nontrivial zeros.
- The critical strip.
- The critical line.
- The relationship between prime numbers and the zeros of the zeta function.

The Riemann hypothesis states that every nontrivial zero of the Riemann zeta function has real part equal to 1/2.

Clearly distinguish established mathematical results from conjectures and experimental observations.

### B. Investigate available datasets

Identify suitable public datasets and downloadable resources.

Look for:

- Lists of prime numbers.
- Tables of known nontrivial zeta zeros.
- High-precision zero computations.
- Existing numerical research resources.
- Mathematical software and libraries.
- Open-source visualisation projects.

Investigate established resources such as LMFDB, OEIS and relevant computational number theory projects.

Determine which datasets can be downloaded and stored locally.

Record their sources, licences, formats, precision and limitations.

Do not download unnecessarily large datasets before establishing what the experiments require.

### C. Develop visual explanations

Investigate what is needed to produce meaningful graphical explanations.

Potential visualisations include:

1. Prime-number distribution.
2. Prime-counting function compared with approximations.
3. The Riemann zeta function in the complex plane.
4. The critical strip and critical line.
5. Nontrivial zeros plotted in the complex plane.
6. The relationship between the imaginary components of zeros and prime-number distribution.
7. The explicit formula connecting primes and zeta zeros.
8. Interactive exploration of the zeta function.

Consider both static scientific figures and interactive visualisations.

### D. Connect the research to the Cosmic Block

Examine the existing Cosmic Block mathematical model.

Recall that its development involves nested integer structures, beginning with zero-initialised arrays and progressing through lines, planes, model spaces and higher-order structures.

Investigate how these structures could represent:

- Numerical sequences.
- Prime-number patterns.
- Complex-valued mathematical quantities.
- Relationships between mathematical objects.
- Transformations and symmetries.
- Higher-dimensional representations of mathematical information.

Do not force a connection where none has been established.

Instead, formulate precise mathematical questions and develop small computational experiments.

A useful research question is:

Can the Cosmic Block provide an alternative computational or visual representation of relationships between prime numbers and the nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function?

### E. Scientific integrity

Our objective is to explain and investigate the Riemann hypothesis, not to pretend that we have proved it.

Every proposed connection must be mathematically defined, computationally reproducible and open to independent scrutiny.

Maintain separate categories for:

- Established mathematical theory.
- Existing computational results.
- Our own experiments.
- New conjectures.
- Unverified interpretations.

The work should eventually support a rigorous academic exposition.
